> **A Professional Multi-Phase Prompting Framework for Video Pre-Planning**
> Built on the Elgendy Academy editing methodology, enhanced with retention science, emotional mapping, and platform intelligence.
> Compatible with: Claude, Gemini, GPT-4, and any advanced LLM.

---

## How to Use This System

### Quick Start
1. **Copy Phase 0** into your LLM conversation → Answer all intake questions
2. **Copy Phase 1** into the SAME conversation → Get your creative strategy
3. **Continue sequentially** through each phase → Each builds on the previous
4. **The final output** is a complete, editor-ready pre-planning document

### Rules
- **Always start with Phase 0** — it shapes everything that follows
- **Keep everything in ONE conversation** — context carries forward
- **Answer intake questions thoroughly** — vague inputs = generic outputs
- **Each phase produces a deliverable** — you can stop at any phase and still have useful output
- **Phases are also modular** — if you already have a script, skip to Phase 2

# PHASE 2: NARRATIVE STRUCTURE DESIGN

> Copy this into the SAME conversation after completing Phase 1.

```
Now let's build the NARRATIVE STRUCTURE. Based on our project brief and creative strategy, design the storytelling backbone of this video. Apply these professional principles:

## STORYTELLING FUNDAMENTALS

Every video, regardless of length, has a narrative arc. Even a 15-second ad tells a micro-story. Apply these principles:

1. **The Hook** (First 3-5 seconds for short content, first 10-15 seconds for long content):
   - What is the visual hook? (The first image that grabs attention)
   - What is the curiosity hook? (The question or mystery planted in the viewer's mind)
   - What is the emotional hook? (The feeling that makes them NEED to keep watching)
   - For ads/social: Would someone STOP SCROLLING for this opening?

2. **The Build** (Rising action — the longest section):
   - How does tension/interest escalate?
   - Where are the "escalation steps"? (Each step raises the stakes or deepens the story)
   - What information is being revealed, and in what ORDER? (Order matters — reveal too much too early = boring; too little = confusing)

3. **The Peak** (Climax — the moment of maximum impact):
   - What is the single most powerful moment in this video?
   - How does everything build toward this moment?
   - What makes this moment hit HARD? (Contrast with what came before? Music drop? Silence? Visual reveal?)

4. **The Resolution** (Landing — how you end):
   - How do you resolve the emotional tension?
   - For ads: Where does the CTA land?
   - For stories: What is the emotional payoff?
   - For social: Does the ending loop back to the beginning? (Loop-ability = replay value)

## OPEN LOOP ARCHITECTURE

Plant unanswered questions early that get resolved later:
- What mystery or question can you plant in the first 5 seconds that won't be answered until later?
- Can you create a visual setup (show something briefly) that pays off later?
- For longer videos: How many open loops can you sustain simultaneously?

## CROSS-CUTTING OPPORTUNITIES

Inspired by the Godfather baptism scene technique — can this video benefit from:
- **Parallel storylines** running simultaneously?
- **Contrasting moments** cut against each other? (Peace vs. chaos, past vs. present, problem vs. solution)
- **Montage sequences** that compress time or show multiple perspectives?

## FORMAT YOUR OUTPUT AS:

- Emotional Hook: [what feeling is triggered]
- Open Loop Planted: [what unanswered question keeps them watching]

**Act 1 — Setup** ([timestamp] - [timestamp]):
- What is established: [setting, characters, context, problem]
- Emotional tone: [starting emotion]
- Information revealed: [what the viewer learns]
- Escalation step: [how interest increases]

**Act 2 — Build** ([timestamp] - [timestamp]):
- Escalation pattern: [how tension/interest rises]
- Key moments: [list the 3-5 most important beats]
- Open loops active: [what questions are keeping viewers engaged]
- Cross-cutting opportunities: [if applicable]
- Mid-point shift: [what changes at the halfway mark to re-engage]

**Act 3 — Peak** ([timestamp] - [timestamp]):

# PHASE 3: RETENTION ENGINEERING

> Copy this into the SAME conversation after completing Phase 2.

---

```
Now let's engineer RETENTION into the narrative structure. This is where we turn a good story into an UNSCROLLABLE viewing experience. Apply these retention science principles to our specific project:

## THE 3-SECOND RULE

Every 3 seconds, something must CHANGE. This doesn't mean a cut — it can be:
- A camera movement beginning or ending
- A text overlay appearing or disappearing
- A sound effect hitting
- A new element entering the frame
- A lighting or color shift
- A music beat or transition
- A speaker's emphasis or gesture

Go through our narrative structure and identify where 3-second stagnation might occur. Flag these as "dead zones."

## PATTERN INTERRUPT SYSTEM

Place deliberate pattern interrupts throughout the video:

For videos UNDER 60 seconds:
- Pattern interrupt every 5-8 seconds
- Types: visual cut, text flash, sound spike, zoom punch, angle change, B-roll insert

For videos 1-5 minutes:
- Pattern interrupt every 12-20 seconds
- Types: topic shift, B-roll sequence, music change, on-screen text, new location, different shot type

For videos 5+ minutes:
- Pattern interrupt every 20-30 seconds
- Types: chapter markers, story shifts, visual variety blocks, re-hooks ("but here's where it gets interesting"), engagement questions

## DROP-OFF PREDICTION

Analyze the narrative structure and predict where viewers are most likely to stop watching:

**High-Risk Drop-Off Points:**
- After the hook resolves (viewers got the curiosity answer too early)
- During long exposition without visual variety
- When energy plateaus (same intensity for too long)
- At natural "chapter breaks" (transitions between sections)
- When the message becomes predictable

For each predicted drop-off point, design a specific COUNTER-MEASURE:
- A re-hook phrase or visual
- An unexpected element
- A new open loop
- An energy shift (calm to intense or vice versa)

## MICRO-HOOK ARCHITECTURE

Design micro-hooks — small moments that create forward momentum:

1. **Verbal Micro-Hooks** (for content with narration):
   - "But that's not even the best part..."
   - "Here's what most people get wrong..."
   - "Watch what happens next..."
   - Or visual equivalents — a character's reaction, a setup without payoff

2. **Visual Micro-Hooks**:
   - Brief flash of upcoming content (J-cut the visual before the audio arrives)
   - Zoom into a detail that creates curiosity
   - Split-second glimpse of the climax moment early on

3. **Audio Micro-Hooks**:
   - Riser sound effects building anticipation
   - Music cutting to silence before a big moment
   - Sound from the next scene bleeding into the current one (L-cut / J-cut)

## DOPAMINE RHYTHM PATTERN

Design the reward cycle:
- TENSION (build curiosity) then RELEASE (deliver satisfying answer/visual/moment) then TENSION (new question) then RELEASE
- Map this rhythm across the entire timeline

## FORMAT YOUR OUTPUT AS:

**Micro-Hook Placement**:
| Timestamp | Hook Type | Description | What It Teases |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| [time] | Visual/Audio/Verbal | [description] | [what keeps them watching] |

**Dopamine Rhythm Map**:
(Show a visual timeline of tension and reward points across the video duration)

1. **[Timestamp]**: [Why they'd leave] then [How we keep them]

```
Now let's build the COMPLETE VISUAL STORYBOARD with integrated sound design. This is the master blueprint — the document an editor could pick up and start cutting from. Apply ALL of the following principles:

## SHOT LANGUAGE RULES

Each shot type carries emotional and narrative meaning. Use them intentionally:

- **Establishing Shot / Extreme Wide**: Sets context — WHERE are we? WHEN is this? What's the WORLD?
- **Wide Shot**: Shows the subject in their environment. Establishes relationship between character and space.
- **Medium Shot**: The workhorse shot. Human interaction, dialogue, activity. Shows upper body + hands.
- **Close-Up**: Emotion, detail, importance. "Look at THIS." Creates intimacy with the subject.
- **Extreme Close-Up**: Maximum intensity. Sweat on skin, eyes, critical details. Use sparingly for impact.
- **Over-the-Shoulder**: Creates perspective. "We're seeing through someone's eyes."
- **Point-of-View (POV)**: Immersive. The viewer IS the character.

**Rule**: You MUST vary shot types. Three consecutive shots of the same type = visual monotony = viewer leaves.

## CAMERA ANGLE MEANING

- **Eye Level**: Neutral, natural, relatable
- **Low Angle** (looking up): Power, dominance, grandeur, aspiration
- **High Angle** (looking down): Vulnerability, smallness, overview, weakness
- **Dutch Angle** (tilted): Unease, tension, disorientation, something is "wrong"
- **Overhead / Bird's Eye**: God-view, patterns, context, artistic
- **Worm's Eye** (extreme low): Extreme power, dramatic impact, surreal

## CAMERA MOVEMENT PURPOSE

- **Static**: Stability, observation, letting the content breathe
- **Pan** (horizontal rotation): Revealing space, following action, connecting elements
- **Tilt** (vertical rotation): Revealing height, showing scale, dramatic reveal
- **Dolly/Push In**: Drawing viewer INTO the moment, increasing intimacy
- **Pull Out**: Revealing context, creating distance, ending a moment
- **Tracking/Follow**: Movement with subject, energy, journey, progression
- **Crane/Jib**: Scale, grandeur, transition between ground and aerial
- **Orbit**: Showcasing, 360 degree attention, dramatic circling
- **Zoom In**: Quick focus, intensity, emphasis (use sparingly)
- **Crash Zoom**: Surprise, comedy, shock — very specific use
- **Handheld**: Rawness, urgency, documentary feel, chaos
- **Steadicam/Gimbal**: Smooth follow, professional movement, guided journey

## TRANSITION MOTIVATION RULES

**CRITICAL PRINCIPLE: Every transition must be MOTIVATED by the story. Never use a transition for decoration.**

- **Hard Cut**: Default. Clean. Used 90%+ of the time. Trust it.
- **Cut on Action**: Cut during movement — seamless, invisible, professional
- **Match Cut**: Visual similarity connects two different scenes (shape, movement, color)
- **Cross-Cutting**: Two parallel storylines intercut — shows simultaneity, builds tension
- **J-Cut**: Audio from next scene starts before the visual — creates anticipation, smooth flow
- **L-Cut**: Audio from previous scene continues into next visual — maintains continuity
- **Jump Cut**: Same angle, skip in time — energy, pace, YouTube-standard for talking heads
- **Smash Cut**: Abrupt contrast — silence to chaos, calm to intense. Impact tool.
- **Cross Dissolve**: Time passage, connection between related scenes. NOT for covering bad edits.
- **Fade to/from Black**: Chapter ending, major time passage, finality. Use sparingly.
- **Invisible Cut**: Hidden transition (whip pan, object passing camera, darkness) — cinematic magic

**NEVER use**: Star wipes, page turns, random preset transitions, or any transition that calls attention to itself without narrative reason.

## SOUND DESIGN INTEGRATION

For EACH shot, specify:
- **Music Layer**: What the music is doing at this moment (building, dropping, silent, steady, transitioning)
- **SFX Layer**: Specific sound effects (whoosh, impact, ambient, riser, bass drop, silence)
- **VO Layer**: If narration exists, what is being said during this shot?
- **Ambient Layer**: Environmental sounds (crowd, wind, traffic, birds, room tone)

## COGNITIVE LOAD SCORING

Rate each shot's information density (1-5):
- **1**: Simple — one subject, no text, familiar setting
- **2**: Light — one subject with minor new element
- **3**: Medium — new information being introduced
- **4**: Heavy — multiple new elements simultaneously
- **5**: Overload — too much (AVOID or break into multiple shots)

**Rule**: Never place two 4+ shots consecutively. After a 4, insert a 1-2 "breathing" shot.

## FORMAT YOUR OUTPUT AS:

| **Camera Angle** | [eye level / low / high / dutch / overhead / worm's eye] |
| **Camera Movement** | [static / pan / tilt / dolly in / pull out / tracking / orbit / zoom / handheld / steadicam] |
| **Description** | [What the viewer SEES — be specific and visual] |
| **Purpose** | [WHY this shot exists — what it communicates to the story] |
| **Emotion Target** | [What the viewer should FEEL during this shot] |
| **Transition IN** | [How we arrived at this shot from the previous one] |
| **Transition OUT** | [How we leave this shot to the next one] |
| **Music** | [What the music is doing] |
| **SFX** | [Specific sound effects] |

| **Text Overlay** | [On-screen text, if any] |

```
Now let's build the PACING & RHYTHM MAP. This is the temporal and musical blueprint that ensures the video FEELS right — that every cut, every beat, every silence lands with precision. Apply these principles:

## TEMPO & RHYTHM PRINCIPLES

1. **Cut on Beat**: Cuts aligned with musical beats create satisfying, almost subconscious rhythm. Identify the BPM of the music and map cut points to beat markers.

2. **Escalating Tempo**: The cut frequency should INCREASE as tension builds:
   - Calm sections: 3-5 second shots
   - Building sections: 2-3 second shots
   - Peak sections: 0.5-1.5 second shots
   - Resolution: Return to longer shots (3-5s)

3. **The Quiet-Loud Pattern**: Alternate between calm and intense moments:
   - QUIET then LOUD then QUIET then LOUDER then QUIET then LOUDEST then QUIET (resolution)
   - Never sustain high intensity for more than 15 seconds without a breathing moment
   - Never sustain low intensity for more than 20 seconds without an energy boost

4. **Music-Edit Sync Points**: Critical moments where the edit MUST align with the music:
   - First beat drop
   - Musical build-ups and releases
   - Vocal emphasis points
   - Instrument entries/exits
   - Final note/beat

5. **The Power of Silence**: Strategic moments of NO music, NO SFX — just raw content:
   - Before a major reveal (0.5-2 seconds of silence amplifies impact)
   - After an emotional peak (silence lets the moment breathe)
   - During intimate/authentic moments (removes artificiality)

6. **Lip Sync & Motion Sync**: When content includes talking or physical movement:
   - Cuts should land on natural speech pauses, not mid-word
   - Physical movements (hand gestures, turns, steps) are natural cut points
   - Camera movement should match subject movement energy

## ENERGY LEVEL TRACKING

Map the energy level across the entire timeline:
- Level 1: Static, ambient, contemplative
- Level 2: Gentle movement, soft engagement
- Level 3: Active, engaging, forward momentum
- Level 4: High energy, fast, exciting
- Level 5: Maximum intensity, rapid cuts, peak moment

# APPENDIX B: TRANSITION REFERENCE

| Transition | When to Use | When to AVOID | Motivation Required |
|------------|-------------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Hard Cut** | Default. Always safe. 90%+ of your cuts. | Never — this is always appropriate | Minimal — just needs basic continuity |
| **Cut on Action** | During physical movement (door opening, turning, hitting) | When there's no movement to cut on | Movement must continue naturally across the cut |
| **Match Cut** | Connecting two scenes through visual similarity | When the visual match is weak or forced | Strong visual/shape/movement similarity |
| **Cross-Cutting** | Parallel events happening simultaneously | When events aren't related or simultaneous | Both storylines must be connected thematically |
| **Jump Cut** | Fast-pacing, time compression, talking head energy | Narrative films, cinematic content, smooth storytelling | Intentional time skip within same scene |
| **J-Cut** | Smooth transitions, building anticipation for next scene | When current scene's visual ending is important | Audio of next scene creates natural curiosity |
| **L-Cut** | Maintaining audio continuity, smooth exit from scene | When you need a clean break between scenes | Current audio naturally extends into next visual |
| **Smash Cut** | Maximum contrast/shock (silence to chaos, calm to intense) | When subtlety is needed | Extreme tonal contrast between scenes |
| **Cross Dissolve** | Time passage, connection between related scenes, dreamlike | Covering up bad edits, random use, between unrelated shots | Thematic connection or temporal transition |
| **Fade to/from Black** | Major time passage, chapter endings, opening/closing | Overuse (more than 2-3 per video), minor transitions | Major narrative division |
| **Invisible Cut** | Cinematic wow moments, seamless location changes | When you want the audience to notice the edit | Physical element masks the cut (whip pan, object, darkness) |
| **Wipe/Slide** | Almost never in modern editing | Modern professional content (looks dated) | Very specific stylistic choices only |

# APPENDIX C: CONTENT-TYPE RULESETS

## Commercial / Ad (15-60s)
- Hook in first 2-3 seconds — non-negotiable
- Message must be clear even with sound off
- "Quiet-Loud" pattern for emotional ads, "All-Loud" for hype ads
- CTA in final 3-5 seconds
- Brand reveal timing: early (0-3s) for awareness, late (last 5s) for storytelling
- Match cuts and cross-cutting for visual sophistication
- Average shot duration: 1.5-3 seconds
- Cuts per minute: 20-40

## YouTube Long-Form (5-30min)
- Hook in first 15 seconds with open loop
- Re-hook every 2-3 minutes
- Pattern interrupt every 20-30 seconds
- Chapter-based structure with clear transitions
- B-roll inserts every 10-15 seconds during talking segments
- Jump cuts standard for talking head portions
- Text overlays for key points
- Average shot duration: 3-8 seconds (varies by section)
- Cuts per minute: 8-20

## Short-Form Reel / TikTok (15-60s)
- Hook in first 0.5-1 second
- Loop-friendly ending (connects back to start)
- Text overlays assume sound-off viewing
- Vertical format (9:16) considerations
- Trending audio awareness
- Average shot duration: 0.5-2 seconds
- Cuts per minute: 30-60+
- Every 3-5 seconds needs a visual change

## Corporate / Brand (1-5min)
- Professional tone, clean edits
- Logo integration within first 10 seconds
- Moderate pacing — not too fast, not too slow
- Authoritative music (no trendy beats)
- Talking head + B-roll structure common
- Subtitles recommended
- Average shot duration: 3-5 seconds
- Cuts per minute: 12-20

## Documentary (5-60min)
- Interview-driven with B-roll storytelling
- Natural pacing respects the subject
- Ambient sound often more important than music
- Lower thirds for speaker identification
- Slower cuts allowed — content drives pacing
- Trust-building through authentic moments
- Average shot duration: 4-10 seconds
- Cuts per minute: 6-15

## Event Highlight (1-5min)
- Establishing shot of venue first
- Chronological or curated best-moments structure
- Mix of wide context shots and tight detail shots
- Energy builds throughout
- Key moments get slow-motion or emphasis
- Music-driven editing (cut on beat)
- Average shot duration: 2-4 seconds
- Cuts per minute: 15-30

## Product Showcase (30s-3min)
- Hero shot of product within first 3 seconds
- Detail shots from multiple angles
- Smooth camera movement (orbit, dolly, tracking)
- Clean background, professional lighting
- Feature callouts with text overlays
- Music: subtle, premium, non-distracting
- Average shot duration: 2-4 seconds
- Cuts per minute: 15-25

## Real Estate / Property (1-3min)
- Aerial establishing shot first
- Flow-through structure (arrival then exterior then entrance then room by room then highlight features then outro)
- Smooth gimbal/steadicam movement throughout
- Natural lighting emphasis
- Minimal text — let visuals speak
- Music: elegant, ambient, premium
- Average shot duration: 3-6 seconds
- Cuts per minute: 10-18

# APPENDIX E: SOUND DESIGN REFERENCE

## Music Arc Templates

### Template 1: The Build (Most Common — Ads, Hype Content)
Ambient pad then add light percussion then add melody then build drums then full arrangement then drop/peak then resolve to ambient

### Template 2: The Emotional Wave (Storytelling, Brand Films)
Piano solo then add strings then swell to full orchestra then pull back to piano then rebuild with choir then climax then single piano note

### Template 3: The Steady Driver (Corporate, Tutorial, Product)
Consistent mid-energy track then slight build at key moments then consistent then gentle peak then return to steady then fade out

### Template 4: The Contrast (Dramatic, Documentary)
Silence then sudden full track then cut to silence then rebuild slowly then contrast again then final sustained note then silence

## Sound Effect Categories

| SFX Type | When to Use | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Whoosh** | Fast transitions, swipe movements, quick cuts | Scene changes, text animations |
| **Impact/Hit** | Text appearing, logo reveal, smash cuts | Title cards, dramatic reveals |
| **Riser** | Building anticipation before a reveal | Pre-climax, before CTA, before reveal |
| **Bass Drop** | Peak moments, after a build | Climax, product reveal, key moment |
| **Click/Tick** | Text appearing letter by letter, counting, precision | Data points, statistics, clean reveals |
| **Ambient** | Establishing mood, location context | City noise, nature, room tone, crowd |
| **Silence** | Before major impact, after emotional peak | Strategic pause for maximum contrast |
| **Reverse** | Rewinding time, going back, undo | Before/after reveals, time reversal |
| **Glitch** | Tech content, disruption, error, digital aesthetic | Tech products, creative transitions |
| **Foley** | Footsteps, handling objects, physical actions | Realistic scenes, product interaction |

## Audio Layering Guide

| Moment Type | Layers Active | Mix Priority |
|-------------|---------------|-------------|
| Hook/Opening | Music (low) + SFX (high) + Ambient | SFX punch above everything |
| Dialogue/VO | VO (dominant) + Music (bed, -12dB) + Light SFX | VO above Music above SFX |
| B-Roll Sequence | Music (prominent) + SFX (supporting) + Ambient | Music above SFX above Ambient |
| Emotional Peak | Music (full) + SFX (impact) | Music and SFX equal |
| Silence Moment | Nothing (or very quiet ambient) | Silence IS the design |
| CTA/Ending | Music (resolving) + VO if applicable | Equal balance |

# APPENDIX F: COMMON MISTAKES CHECKLIST

Use this as a final check against the most common editing errors (sourced from Elgendy Academy + industry standards):

### OPENING MISTAKES
- Starting with a logo animation (nobody cares about your logo — hook first)
- Starting with black screen or slow fade-in (viewers leave before anything happens)
- Generic establishing shot without purpose (drone shot of city = meaningless)
- Opening shot held too long (more than 3 seconds without movement or change)

### TRANSITION MISTAKES
- Using transitions to cover bad edits (cross dissolve is not a band-aid)
- Preset transition packs that look cheap/dated (star wipes, page curls, 3D spins)
- Same transition repeated without motivation (not every cut needs a whoosh)
- Hard transition sounds (swoosh SFX too loud, breaks the flow)
- Transitions that don't match the video's style (cinematic video with meme transitions)

### PACING MISTAKES
- Single shot held for 5+ seconds without movement or purpose (dead zone)
- Same energy level for more than 20 seconds (monotone = boring)
- No quiet moments (all loud = nothing feels loud)
- Peak section has the same cut rate as the setup (no escalation)
- Resolution section is as fast as the peak (jarring, no emotional landing)

### AUDIO MISTAKES
- Music with watermark (demo version from stock site)
- Audio levels inconsistent (some shots loud, some quiet)
- Wrong music mood for content (upbeat music on sad content)
- No sound design at all (just music + VO, no SFX or ambient)
- Music overpowers voiceover (can't hear the speaker)
- Audio clipping / distortion in peaks

### VISUAL MISTAKES
- Same shot type repeated 3+ times consecutively (visual monotony)
- Shaky footage without stabilization (looks amateur)
- Mixed quality footage (4K next to 720p)
- Low-quality stock footage that doesn't match original footage
- Drone/aerial footage that's unstable or drifting
- Color grading inconsistency (warm to cool to warm without motivation)

### STORYTELLING MISTAKES
- Story reveals everything too early (no reason to keep watching)
- No clear message (viewer finishes confused about the point)
- Style changes mid-video without reason (started cinematic, ended vlog-style)
- Unmotivated effects (lens flares, light leaks, glitch effects for no reason)
- B-roll that doesn't relate to what's being said
- Text overlays blocking important visual content
- Missing CTA when the video is designed to drive action

### PLATFORM MISTAKES
- Wrong aspect ratio for the platform
- No subtitles on a platform where 50%+ watch without sound
- Duration exceeds platform sweet spot
- Pacing too slow for the platform's audience behavior
- Content not optimized for mobile viewing (text too small, details too fine)
